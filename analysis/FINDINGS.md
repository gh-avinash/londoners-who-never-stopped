## 0. The story in one paragraph (current evidence)

When London locked down in spring 2020, the network fell to **~6%** of normal — but not evenly. Stations whose surrounding neighbourhoods have more **frontline workers** (carers, drivers, cleaners, security, shop and hospitality staff) kept **more than twice** the share of riders of those in work-from-home neighbourhoods (11.4% vs 5.0%), and stations next to major **hospitals** kept more riders than their own neighbours in 13 of 15 cases. Six years on, London is back to **~80%** of 2019 like-for-like — but **central London (zone 1) has recovered least (~76%)**, outer London most (~86%), and **Friday is the emptiest weekday** everywhere, most of all in the centre (~71%). The people who could not stop in 2020 are the people whose travel came back; the hybrid-work recovery is the one that stayed partial.

## 3. Method & assumptions

### 3a. Lockdown retention

Script: [`scripts/lockdown_retention.py`](scripts/lockdown_retention.py) → [`processed/lockdown_retention.csv`](processed/lockdown_retention.csv) (by footfall name). Same rules re-implemented per place in [`scripts/recovery.py`](scripts/recovery.py) → [`processed/station_story.csv`](processed/station_story.csv).

**Metric:** retention = average weekday footfall (entries + exits) during lockdown ÷ average weekday footfall in the same calendar weeks of 2019.

| #   | Assumption                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Status        | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A1  | **Lockdown window = 30 Mar – 15 May 2020.**                                                                                                                                                                                 | ⚠️ judgement  | Starts one week after the 23 Mar stay-at-home order (lets behaviour settle); ends before easing on 13 May took effect.                                                                |
| A2  | **Baseline = 1 Apr – 17 May 2019** (same calendar weeks, one year earlier).                                                                                                                                                 | ⚠️ judgement  | Same season removes Easter/weather effects.                                                                                                                                           |
| A3  | **Weekdays only; bank holidays excluded** in both periods (19 & 22 Apr, 6 May 2019; 10 & 13 Apr, 8 May 2020).                                                                                                               | ✅            | Weekends behave differently and would blur the commuter signal.                                                                                                                       |
| A4  | **Footfall = entries + exits.**                                                                                                                                                                                             | ⚠️ judgement  | Counts both directions of travel at a station.                                                                                                                                        |
| A5  | **Stations with < 1,000 baseline daily taps are dropped.**                                                                                                                                                                  | ⚠️ judgement  | Percentages are noisy for very small stations.                                                                                                                                        |
| A6  | **Stations with < 50 daily taps in lockdown are treated as "closed by TfL"**, not ranked.                                                                                                                                   | ✅ largely    | Matches known closures (e.g. Covent Garden, Temple, Charing Cross). A few may have been near-closed rather than formally closed.                                                      |
| A7  | **Baseline cross-check:** each station's spring-2019 baseline is compared with Feb 2020 (3–28 Feb, weekdays). If they differ by more than 25%, the station is marked `unstable_baseline` and **excluded from the ranking**. | ✅            | We can't tell which "normal" is right for those stations, so we don't pick one.                                                                                                       |
| A8  | Station names are used as the join key between years.                                                                                                                                                                       | ✅ checked    | No 2019 name disappears by 2026. New names are only new stations or Elizabeth line entries ("X EL" renamed "X Elizabeth Line" on 28 May 2025). Handled by grouping into places (A11). |
| A9  | Taps measure **where** people travelled, not **who** they were or why.                                                                                                                                                      | ⚠️ key caveat | Tested indirectly with census data (F7) and hospitals (F4). Still correlation, not proof of who the riders were.                                                                      |

## 4. Findings

### F1 — The network nearly stopped ✅

Across ranked stations, weekday footfall in strict lockdown was **5.8%** of the 2019 level (5.3% including closed stations). Median station: 6.8%. 10th percentile 3.4%, 90th percentile 12.5%.

### F2 — But it did not stop evenly ✅

| Kept moving       | Retention | Emptied                 | Retention |
| ----------------- | --------- | ----------------------- | --------- |
| Dagenham Heathway | 17.8%     | Bank                    | 1.3%      |
| Barking           | 17.7%     | Piccadilly Circus       | 1.4%      |
| Hampstead Heath   | 17.4%     | Monument                | 1.6%      |
| Goodmayes         | 16.4%     | Oxford Circus           | 1.9%      |
| Dagenham East     | 16.3%     | Tottenham Court Road    | 2.0%      |
| Becontree         | 14.9%     | King's Cross St Pancras | 2.3%      |
| Canning Town      | 14.1%     | Liverpool Street        | 2.7%      |
| West Ham          | 14.1%     | Farringdon              | 2.7%      |
| Northwick Park    | 14.0%     | Canary Wharf DLR        | 2.9%      |

- Barking kept **~13×** the share of its riders that Bank did (17.7% vs 1.3%).
- In absolute terms, Barking (~9,900/day) had more people still travelling than **Liverpool Street (~5,800) and Bank (~2,000) combined**.
- Top results agree under both baselines, usually within 1 percentage point.

### F3 — Geography of who kept moving ✅

High-retention stations cluster in **outer east London** (Barking & Dagenham, Newham, Redbridge) and **north-west London** (Brent, Harrow). Lowest retention: the City, West End, Canary Wharf, major rail termini.

### F4 — Stations next to hospitals kept more riders than their neighbours ✅ (upgraded from ⚠️)

16 of 32 acute hospitals have a ranked station within 800 m. 15 could be compared with their neighbours (Romford had too few):

- **13 of 15** hospital stations kept more riders than the median of their non-hospital neighbours. Median difference **+2.2 points**. Sign test **p = 0.004** (very unlikely by chance).
- Biggest: **Hampstead Heath** (Royal Free) +8.8 pts, **Whitechapel** (Royal London) +8.4, **Denmark Hill** (King's College) +7.1, **Warren Street** (UCH) +4.8, **Tooting Broadway** (St George's) +3.9.
- The 2 exceptions: **Paddington** (St Mary's, −3.3) — a major rail terminus whose commuter collapse swamps hospital traffic; **Hanwell** (Ealing Hospital, −0.4, 707 m away).
- Caveat: hospital neighbourhoods may also have more frontline residents; the neighbour comparison reduces but doesn't eliminate this.

### F5 — 41 stations were closed during lockdown ✅

Arsenal, Barbican, Bayswater, Bermondsey, Blackhorse Road, Borough, Bounds Green, Bow Road, Caledonian Road, Chalk Farm, Chancery Lane, Charing Cross, Clapham South, Covent Garden, Gloucester Road, Goodge Street, Great Portland Street, Hampstead, Heathrow T2&3 / T4 / T5 (TfL Rail/HEx), Holland Park, Hyde Park Corner, Kilburn Park, Lancaster Gate, Manor House, Mansion House, Mornington Crescent, Pimlico, Queensway, Redbridge, Regents Park, South Wimbledon, Southwark, St James's Park, Stepney Green, Swiss Cottage, Temple, Tufnell Park, Warwick Avenue, West India Quay.

### F6 — Some stations have unreliable baselines and are excluded ✅

- **Lockdown analysis (7):** White Hart Lane, Cyprus, Leyton Midland Road, Upper Holloway, Harringay Green Lanes, Leytonstone High Road, South Tottenham.
- **Recovery analysis (9, autumn 2019 vs Feb 2020):** Crouch Hill, Harringay Green Lanes, Leyton Midland Road, Leytonstone High Road, South Tottenham, Upper Holloway, Walthamstow Queen's Road, Wanstead Park, Woodgrange Park — **all on the Overground Gospel Oak–Barking line**.
- **Gospel Oak–Barking line:** footfall fell ~30–40% in Jan–Feb 2020, before Covid (e.g. South Tottenham ~3,000/day in spring 2019 → 1,840 in Feb 2020). ⚠️ **Cause still not confirmed** — a web search found the line's well-documented 2019 train shortage and one Jan 2020 weekend closure, but nothing explaining weekday falls in early 2020. Not blocking: the stations are excluded either way.
- **White Hart Lane:** roughly doubled from autumn 2019 (~3,600 → ~6,300/day). 🌐 **Confirmed:** the rebuilt station's new entrance opened 26 Aug 2019 (official opening 11 Sep 2019), adding a new ticket hall and step-free access, next to the new Tottenham Hotspur stadium. ([TfL press release](https://nile.tfl.gov.uk/info-for/media/press-releases/2019/august/a-new-look-white-hart-lane-station-welcomes-customers), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Hart_Lane_railway_station))

### F7 — Census confirms: neighbourhoods that couldn't work from home kept travelling ✅ (key finding)

Spearman correlation between station lockdown retention and the station's surrounding census areas:

| Census measure (2021)                  | Expected | All stations (n=358) | Zones 3+ (n=191) | Zones 4+ (n=128) |
| -------------------------------------- | -------- | -------------------- | ---------------- | ---------------- |
| Worked mainly from home                | −        | **−0.58**            | **−0.61**        | **−0.67**        |
| Frontline occupations (groups 6, 8, 9) | +        | **+0.62**            | **+0.66**        | **+0.69**        |
| Managers & professionals (groups 1, 2) | −        | **−0.61**            | **−0.61**        | **−0.62**        |
| Health & social care industry          | +        | +0.41                | +0.24            | +0.32            |

- All four measures point the way the story predicts, and the occupation/WFH links are **strong** (|ρ| ≈ 0.6–0.7) — and get stronger in residential outer London, where the census residents really are the station's riders.
- In zones 3+, stations in the **most frontline** fifth of neighbourhoods (36% frontline, 23% WFH) kept **11.4%** of riders; the **least frontline** fifth (13% frontline, 55% WFH) kept **5.0%** — **2.3× as much**.
- Health & social care alone is a weaker link than occupation overall: "key workers" are much broader than NHS staff (drivers, cleaners, care, retail, security).
- Caveat: area-level correlation (ecological) — it shows that places with more frontline residents kept moving, not that each rider was a frontline worker.

### F8 — London has recovered to ~80%, unevenly ✅

Weekday autumn footfall vs autumn 2019, like-for-like (361 places):

| 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026    |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ------- |
| 36%  | 59%  | 74%  | 79%  | 80%  | 80%  | **80%** |

- **Recovery has plateaued at ~80% since 2024.**
- By zone, 2026:

|           | Overall | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri     |
| --------- | ------- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ------- |
| Zone 1    | **76%** | 74% | 78% | 79% | 79% | **71%** |
| Zones 2–3 | 82%     | 80% | 83% | 84% | 84% | 80%     |
| Zones 4+  | **86%** | 85% | 88% | 87% | 87% | 83%     |

- **Robustness:** excluding stations near the Elizabeth line changes these by < 0.5 pts. Including the Elizabeth line and every flagged station **in every zone** (only unstable baselines dropped): Zone 1 **83% (Fri 76%)**, Zones 2–3 87%, Zones 4+ **95%**. So the centre–outer gap holds on both bases (10 pts like-for-like, 13 pts all-in) and so does the Friday dip. _(Corrected 2026-10-28: earlier text compared the all-in Zone 1 figure against the like-for-like outer figure.)_
- Network-wide like-for-like: 80% overall, **Fri 76%**, Mon 78%, Tue 82%. The 71% Friday figure is **Zone 1 only**.
- **Friday is the emptiest weekday in every zone**, and the dip is deepest in zone 1 (8 pts below Thursday). Monday is the second-emptiest. Tue–Thu is the "office week".
- Positive correlation between lockdown retention and 2026 recovery is weak (ρ = +0.24): places that kept moving in 2020 tend to be the more-recovered ones, but the zone pattern is the clearer signal.

### F9 — The Elizabeth line is the biggest single change since 2019 ✅

The 32 Elizabeth line places average **121%** of 2019 (e.g. Abbey Wood 343%, Hayes & Harlington 211%, Forest Gate 172%, Farringdon 164%, Tottenham Court Road 155%). This is new capacity, not "recovery" — but it's a possible positive side-story for the "Future" section.

### F10 — Least-recovered big stations are mostly central (2026, ≥ 10k/day in 2019, like-for-like) ✅ / ⚠️

Lancaster Gate 42%, Chancery Lane 50% (**Fri 40%**), Barbican 51%, Southwark 51%, Regent's Park 53%, Monument 57%, St Paul's 59%, Old Street 60%, Elephant & Castle 61%, Holborn 62%.
⚠️ Some of these are probably losing riders to nearby Elizabeth line entrances (Barbican → Farringdon; Holborn/Chancery Lane → Tottenham Court Road/Farringdon; Lancaster Gate → Paddington/Bond Street), so **use the zone-level numbers (F8) for headlines, not individual station rankings**.

### F11 — "What if travel only followed jobs?" A jobs-only model explains about a third ✅

Script: `scripts/build_app_data.py` (model fitted there and shipped to the wall's simulator).

- Linear model across 358 ranked stations: **lockdown retention ≈ 2.31 + 0.257 × frontline share (%)**, **R² = 0.35**. Where people's jobs are explains about a third of the station-to-station difference.
- **Biggest positive surprises** (kept moving more than local jobs predict): Hampstead Heath +13.2 pts (Royal Free), Crouch Hill +11.9, West Silvertown +9.6, Kilburn High Road +8.4, Bethnal Green +7.9, Kenton +7.9, Brondesbury +6.7, **Northwick Park +6.6 (hospital)**, Goodmayes +6.5, **Denmark Hill +6.4 (King's College Hospital)**.
- **Biggest negative surprises:** Heathrow T4 −8.8, T2&3 −8.5, **London City Airport −7.9**, Heathrow T5 −7.4, New Cross −7.3, King's Cross St Pancras −5.4. **Airports** collapse because flying stopped — local jobs can't keep them moving.
- Hospital stations' residuals: median **+1.3** vs overall median −0.2 — consistent with F4.
- ⚠️ Caveats: one-variable linear model, residents ≠ riders (A26); used to _illustrate_ not to predict.
